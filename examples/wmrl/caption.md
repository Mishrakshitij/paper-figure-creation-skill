**World-model rewards with an anchor for correction.** The agent samples groups of candidate research trajectories. All groups receive predicted rewards; a small anchor subset also receives rewards from real execution. Matched score pairs fit a monotone map online, correcting predictions before group-relative advantages are formed. Real rewards on anchor groups and calibrated rewards on other groups produce separate gradient sums. Inverse-variance weights combine them into the policy update. Here `A` is a group-relative advantage; `G_E` and `G_WM` are the anchor and other group sets; `g_E` and `g_WM` are their summed gradient estimates; and `V_E`, `V_WM` are the corresponding variance quantities. The displayed fusion is the conceptual form of Equation (5); the paper estimates its relative weighting from calibrated anchor residuals. Multi-turn trajectory details and variance-estimation warmup are omitted. The task and code are illustrative. The approximately 10% anchor setting and method relations are from [WMRL v3, Sections 3.1–3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12564v3).

With `w_E = 1/V_E` and `w_WM = 1/V_WM`, the normalization is

$$\hat g=\frac{w_Eg_E+w_{WM}g_{WM}}{w_E|G_E|+w_{WM}|G_{WM}|}.$$

**Alt text.** Three stages run from left to right. An agent generates code candidates grouped into sets. The central stage sends anchor groups through both a real sandbox and a world model, collecting paired rewards to fit an online monotone calibration. Other groups use only world-model scores and enter a separate application operation, which applies the fitted map. Two streams on the right use true anchor rewards and corrected other-group rewards, respectively. Their gradient sums receive inverse-variance multipliers, are summed and normalized, and update the policy.
