**Masked autoencoder pretraining through one illustrative example.** (a) Twelve of sixteen patches are masked, leaving four visible patch tokens with their original position information. (b) Only the visible tokens enter the encoder. (c) The encoded tokens are projected to the decoder representation; copies of a shared learned mask token fill the missing positions. The inset shows restoration from a shuffled visible-token order to the original spatial order, followed by decoder position embeddings. A lightweight decoder predicts patch pixels, and the reconstruction objective compares only masked positions with targets from the original image. The block stacks indicate architectural asymmetry, not literal layer counts. Patch projection layers, the CLS token, and internal attention operations are omitted. All depicted scene content and reconstruction are original schematics, not measured model outputs. The figure covers pretraining; downstream recognition is outside its scope. Based on [He et al., Section 3 and Figure 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377v3) and the [official implementation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/mae/blob/main/models_mae.py).

Alt text: A geometric tree landscape is divided into sixteen patches. Patches 2, 6, 11, and 15 remain visible, and their contents appear in a packed sequence in the order 11, 2, 15, 6. A larger encoder processes this short sequence. A restored grid combines the four encoded tokens with twelve shared mask tokens before a smaller decoder produces an illustrative reconstruction. An inset maps shuffled visible tokens and mask-token copies back to positions 1 through 16. Original pixel targets are retained only in the twelve masked locations and feed the masked-only MSE loss together with the reconstruction.
